# Managing Variance and Consistency

Source: [VGCguide.com](https://www.vgcguide.com) — Teambuilding and Battling sections. Primary authors: Wolfe Glick, Aaron Traylor.

## Luck Is a Skill

Competitive Pokemon functions like poker: variance exists, but elite players win consistently through superior decision-making. Wolfe Glick: "In Pokemon, luck is a skill, so getting bad luck normally means you made a mistake in teambuilding or playing."

The best players structure their teams and gameplay to control WHEN and HOW luck manifests, ensuring variance appears during favorable moments rather than critical ones.

## Your Team Determines Your Luck

Three ways to improve your luck through teambuilding:

### 1. Use Accurate Moves

A 90% accuracy move used once per game across 30 tournament games means ~3 losses from misses alone. Multiple inaccurate moves compound the risk.

Wolfe's personal framework: soft threshold at 90% accuracy; never go below willingly.

| Accurate | Powerful | When to Use Powerful |
|---|---|---|
| Thunderbolt | Thunder | Weather (rain), or carry both |
| Flamethrower | Fire Blast | When the KO matters and accurate version falls short |
| Scald | Hydro Pump | No real substitute (Rotom-Wash) |
| Dragon Pulse | Draco Meteor | Carry both when possible |

Reasons to accept lower accuracy:
- Pokemon carries both accurate and powerful versions
- No real substitute exists (Precipice Blades)
- Accuracy can be boosted (Gravity, Dynamax)
- Move isn't relied on to win but offers high upside (Sleep Powder, Will-O-Wisp)

### 2. Act First

Moving before opponents prevents secondary effects — flinch, paralysis, burn, freeze, crits. "You can't get unlucky if your opponent never gets to make any moves."

Good speed control = better luck. A team with all slow Pokemon will be "unluckier" than one with good speed distribution.

### 3. Build Resilient Teams

- Very fast/frail teams are vulnerable to domino effects from single bad-luck events
- Overly defensive teams lose too much from surprise KOs
- Teams over-reliant on one Pokemon for a matchup are brittle

Example of good luck management: Tapu Fini's Misty Surge prevents random burns/paralysis/freeze. But Calm Mind Tapu Fini creates risk by extending games, giving opponents more chances to land crits.

## Consistency

Consistency means achieving victory regardless of variables in opponents, compositions, decisions, and luck. Three domains:

### Consistent Teambuilding
Use utility moves that reliably counter opponent strategies (Fake Out, Taunt, Haze, Safeguard). Avoid utility moves with narrow activation windows (Lucky Chant).

### Consistent Strategy — Self-Evaluation Checklist
1. Is it theoretically sound?
2. Does it succeed on ladder?
3. Does it cover common tournament threats?
4. Does it have matchup coverage gaps?
5. Does it hold up against repeat opponents?
6. Does it perform against increasingly prepared opponents?

### Consistent Battle Execution
- Find leads effective regardless of opponent choices
- Avoid luck-dependent paths when alternatives exist
- Identify turns where moves work universally
- Secure endgames with near-certainty

## Surprise Factor

Unexpected teambuilding choices provide advantages but carry tradeoffs with consistency.

The core tradeoff: **Consistency** (strength after opponents learn your choices) vs. **Surprise** (impact of unorthodox reveals).

- Best-of-1 strongly favors surprises
- Best-of-3 rewards consistency
- Evaluate long-term viability once opponents know your strategy
- Consider tournament length and likelihood of repeated matchups

"Catching opponents off guard is definitely something to consider, but surprise shouldn't be the primary driving force when starting."

## Dealing with Bad Luck in Battle

Before using any uncertain mechanic, evaluate:
1. Consequence severity if it fails
2. Whether better alternatives exist
3. Whether the benefit justifies the variance

### Status Conditions
Build multiple defensive layers: items (Safety Goggles, Lum Berry), moves (Taunt), type advantages (Grass resists Spore/Sleep Powder). Eliminate status-spreading Pokemon quickly.

### After Bad Luck
- Don't tilt — frustration leads to ill-advised emotional plays
- Experiment with new compositions or take breaks
- Celebrate good decision-making regardless of outcomes
- Separate successful plays from correct plays

## The Luck Audit

When you feel unlucky, ask:
- Did I choose accurate moves, or gamble on 80% options?
- Did I have speed control, or let opponents move first consistently?
- Am I over-reliant on one Pokemon for key matchups?
- Did I give opponents too many turns to get lucky (long stall games)?
- Could I have eliminated their status-spreading Pokemon earlier?

The answer to "bad luck" is almost always a teambuilding adjustment.
