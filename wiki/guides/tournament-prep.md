# Tournament Preparation

Source: [VGCguide.com](https://www.vgcguide.com) — Teambuilding and Battling sections. Primary authors: Wolfe Glick, Aaron Traylor.

## Best of 1 vs Best of 3

Bo1 and Bo3 are fundamentally different formats requiring different approaches.

### Best of 1

**Rewards:** Surprise, fast-paced offense, meta calls.

**Team Preview Rules:**
1. Pick risk-averse leads
2. Cover opponent's strongest combos
3. Have answers to all 6 opponent Pokemon across your 4
4. When uncertain, lean on team strengths

**In Battle:**
- Assume opponents have all strongest attacks
- Identify worst-case scenarios each turn and prevent them
- Accept that Bo1 is volatile — be OK with losing

**Surprise is valuable:** Unconventional sets, unexpected movesets/items, underutilized species, non-standard EV distributions.

### Best of 3

**Rewards:** Consistency, adaptation, multiple modes.

**Key Advantages:**
- Multiple battle modes create guessing games across the series
- Defensive backbones have more breathing room
- Conserving key information until crucial moments differentiates Bo3 play

**Between Games:**
- Analyze which opponent Pokemon caused most difficulty
- Determine if unrevealed team members could help
- Assess opponent's preparedness
- Consider: same strategy (if it worked), alter crucial turn decisions, or entirely change approach

**Information Management:**
- Complete moveset revelation eliminates uncertainty
- Item discoveries reshape evaluation
- Speed stat confirmation changes calculations
- Avoid revealing unnecessary moves/items on underutilized Pokemon

**Teams designed for Bo3 work fine in Bo1, but not vice versa.**

## Choosing a Tournament Team

### Positive Indicators

1. **The team is fun** — enjoyment is a genuine positive signal
2. **You're in control** — numerous options and multiple paths to victory; Pokemon act proactively (forcing responses) rather than reactively
3. **You know your weaknesses** — distinguish "hard but winnable" from "almost no options"
4. **Consistency** — performs reliably even when opponents understand it

### Decision Factors

- **Comfort over cutting-edge** — prefer familiar, well-practiced teams over new builds exploiting current trends
- **Consistency as priority** — rely minimally on surprise factor
- **Alignment with tournament objectives:**
  - Placing/CP goals: standard, low-risk teams
  - Top Cut: similar approach with tighter error margins
  - Winning: consider strategic risks and unique approaches in larger events
- **Tournament size matters:** larger events have greater opponent variety, tighter competition, and extended play favoring faster-paced styles

## When Time Is Running Out

### Pre-Tournament Checklist

1. Build or find a team
2. Finalize EV spreads, moves, and items
3. Get ladder/Bo3 practice — iterate if necessary
4. Breed/catch Pokemon in-game
5. Finalize Pokemon in-game (EVs, items, Hyper Training)
6. Fill out team sheet
7. Double-check all stats on sheet vs. EVs/IVs vs. in-game

### Under Time Pressure

- **Remain calm** — clear-headed decisions are more objective
- **Map remaining tasks against available time**
- **Focus on fundamentals** — break format knowledge into simple concrete statements
- **No magic bullets** — don't search frantically for one Pokemon that answers all threats
- **Beware "grass is greener"** — switching to someone else's team without understanding its weaknesses is dangerous; "being surprised by a shortcoming of your own team is one of the worst things that can happen during a tournament"
- **Play to your strengths** — prioritize experience with Pokemon and strategies
- **A flawed team you know beats a perfect team you don't** — "it's better to have a flawed team that you know how to pilot well rather than a perfect team you're inexperienced with"

## Ladder Ratings

Ladder serves primarily as "proof of concept" for team viability, not as a comprehensive skill metric.

### Showdown ELO Ranges

| Range | Description |
|---|---|
| **1000–1200** | New accounts, unorthodox teams, alts. Build fundamentals. |
| **1200–1400** | More challenging opponents, prediction-based decisions begin. |
| **1400–1600** | Recommended primary practice range. Excellent tournament prep. |
| **Top 500** | Realistic intermediate goal; requires consistently high win rate. |
| **Top 100** | Significantly smaller margin for error. |
| **#1** | Requires extreme perseverance and significant luck due to asymmetric point gains/losses. |

### How to Use Ladder

1. Primary objective: tournament performance
2. Secondary use: ladder as team validation tool
3. Tertiary consideration: ladder ranking numbers

Avoid long ladder sessions after proving team concept viability — redirect focus to practicing best-of-3s.

Don't confuse Bo1 ladder skills with Bo3 tournament skills.
