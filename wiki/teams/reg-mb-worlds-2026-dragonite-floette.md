## Strategy

This team won the 2026 Pokemon Video Game World Championships, piloted by Takuma Yamazaki from Japan in his first Worlds ever. The core innovation is the dual mega pairing of Dragonite and Floette-Eternal, which was not a common duo heading into the tournament. Dragonite's Multiscale bulk makes it nearly impossible to OHKO, and its Dragon Pulse trades favorably into most of the format while Heat Wave punishes Whimsicott, Kingambit, and Scizor. Critically, Dragonite matches up exceptionally well into Mega Charizard — it takes very little damage from Charizard's attacks, and if the opponent mega-evolves Charizard and sets Sun, Dragonite's Heat Wave becomes even stronger.

The team's flexibility was a key factor in winning Worlds. Sneezler's Fake Out plus Feint combination creates a constant guessing game from turn one — opponents must decide whether to Protect into Feint or attack into Fake Out, and guessing wrong often means giving up an immediate knockout. Choice Scarf Garchomp provides speed control without Tailwind, outspeeding even max-speed Mega Aerodactyl. Life Orb Basculegion offers devastating Wave Crash and endgame Last Respects, while Chople Berry Kingambit is specially defensive enough to survive Light of Ruin from timid max SpA Floette-Mega in all but one damage roll.

The team can lead with virtually any combination of two — Sneezler/Garchomp for fast aggression into Aerodactyl teams, Dragonite/Basculegion for bulky offense, Sneezler/Dragonite for Fake Out plus Feint pressure, or Garchomp/Basculegion for immediate burst damage into Raichu compositions. Picking the correct mega evolution between Dragonite and Floette is the most critical team preview decision, as each has different favorable and unfavorable matchups (Dragonite prefers to see Charizard; Floette prefers to see opposing Floette).

**Moves:** Dragon Pulse · Heat Wave · Extreme Speed · Protect

**Role:** Bulky special mega — primary anti-Charizard answer

Dragonite is the star of the team and the reason it won Worlds. Max SpA and max Speed with Modest nature gives it strong offensive pressure while Multiscale makes it nearly impossible to OHKO — in the video, it survived a 4x super-effective Ice Fang plus Rock Slide from Mega Aerodactyl and still stayed on the field. Dragon Pulse cleanly 2HKOs most neutral targets, Heat Wave picks up spread damage and punishes common Steel and Grass types in Sun, and Extreme Speed provides crucial priority to finish off weakened threats. The Charizard matchup is the key selling point: Charizard deals minimal damage to Dragonite, and mega-evolving Charizard sets Sun which boosts Dragonite's Heat Wave. Pairs beautifully with Garchomp since Dragonite is immune to Earthquake.

**Moves:** Close Combat · Dire Claw · Fake Out · Feint

**Role:** Lead disruptor — Fake Out / Feint mind games

The most talked-about Pokemon from the Worlds finals. Fake Out plus Feint creates a devastating turn-one guessing game: opponents who Protect get punished by Feint, and opponents who attack get slowed by Fake Out. This duo favors the Sneasler player more often than not. Close Combat hits Kingambit and Archaludon hard, while Dire Claw provides STAB Poison damage with a chance to inflict status. No Protect means opponents can exploit Sneasler if they read correctly, but the Sash guarantees at least one more action. Pairs especially well with Dragonite leads for Faint plus Dragon Pulse/Heat Wave double-ups on turn one.

**Role:** Physical sweeper — endgame Last Respects win condition

Life Orb Adaptability makes Wave Crash devastating, threatening OHKOs on a huge portion of the format. Last Respects in the endgame is often a checkmate move that nothing can survive. The spread is not max Attack (18 points instead of 32), trading some offensive power for SpD bulk — the SpD investment helps survive modest Mega Charizard Solar Beam reliably and other special attacks. Aqua Jet provides priority that pairs with Dragonite's Extreme Speed for double priority finishes. The HP investment provides reasonable longevity despite Life Orb recoil. Often best saved for the back to clean up with Last Respects once teammates have traded.

**Role:** Bulky tank — specially defensive pivot with four attacks

Max HP with heavy SpD investment makes this Kingambit incredibly tanky on the special side — it survived Heat Wave from Mega Charizard in Sun during the Worlds finals, and the SpD is calibrated to survive Light of Ruin from timid max SpA Floette-Mega in almost every roll. Chople Berry covers the Fighting weakness. No Protect and no speed investment means it relies on positioning and Sucker Punch for priority. Low Kick is the tech for opposing Kingambit and Archaludon. The 70 Speed (no investment) actually allows it to abuse opposing Trick Room, moving before faster Pokemon when Trick Room is set. Four attacks with no Protect is risky but maximizes coverage.

**Moves:** Dragon Claw · Stomping Tantrum · Earthquake · Rock Slide

**Role:** Speed control — Choice Scarf revenge killer

Choice Scarf at 149 Speed (223 effective) outspeeds max-speed Mega Aerodactyl and everything slower. The HP and Defense investment survives +2 Black Glasses Sucker Punch from Kingambit and has a reasonable chance to survive Life Orb Wave Crash from Basculegion. Rough Skin punishes contact moves. Rock Slide provides spread damage with flinch chance — a single flinch under Scarf speed can be game-breaking. Stomping Tantrum covers Steel types and gets boosted damage if the previous attack fails. Pairs excellently with Dragonite, which is immune to Earthquake, enabling safe Earthquake spam. Being locked into a move is the main drawback — Archaludon can switch in and absorb a bad lock with Stamina boosts.

**Moves:** Moonblast · Dazzling Gleam · Light of Ruin · Protect

**Role:** Special nuke — near-max Speed/SpA Fairy attacker

The team's second mega option, brought when Dragonite has unfavorable matchups (e.g., into opposing Floette or Frostlass teams). Near-max Speed and max SpA means Light of Ruin hits incredibly hard — it's the strongest Fairy-type attack in the game and can OHKO even specially defensive targets. Moonblast provides reliable single-target STAB, and Dazzling Gleam gives spread damage. The Def investment with Timid nature means Floette has almost no physical bulk, making it vulnerable to priority and physical attackers. The 8 Defense points provide marginal help but Floette is fundamentally a glass cannon that trades favorably with Light of Ruin's raw power. Protect helps with positioning and scouting.
