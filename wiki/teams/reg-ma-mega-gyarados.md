## Strategy

Mega Gyarados has barely seen play this format, but Nathaniel Sitler used it to reach top 8 at Indianapolis Regionals as the #1 seed going into cut, beating Wolf Glick along the way. It functions really well as a second mega evolution alongside Calm Mind Floette — a slept-on pick that patches matchups Floette alone doesn't love.

Double Fake Out (Incineroar and Sneasler) enables the setup game, Sinistcha adds healing, and Coaching on Sneasler is the standout tech — Fake Out or Coaching straight into Dragon Dance turns Gyarados into a huge offensive threat almost immediately (Coaching also boosts Garchomp's damage and defense, or helps Floette survive physical hits better). The general plan each game is to commit to one of the two mega evolutions and build around it — either leaning into both Fake Out users plus Sinistcha, or dropping one Fake Out user for the extra offense that four-attack Garchomp provides.

Despite being a setup team, this squad is fast at its base — both Floette and Gyarados sit close to max speed even before any boosts, and a single Dragon Dance is enough to outrun most of the format's major threats. Floette follows the same pattern as the rest of this format's Calm Mind Floette builds — get a boost or two off, then Draining Kiss to heal back up — while Gyarados gives the team an Intimidate user on top of everything else. Roseli Berry plus four attacks on Garchomp (including Poison Jab) lets it meaningfully threaten opposing fairies like Sylveon and Mega Floette, something most Garchomp sets can't do.
