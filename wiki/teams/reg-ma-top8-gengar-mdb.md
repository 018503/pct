## Strategy

If you want to run Mega Gengar but not lean on Perish Song, this top 8 team built by MDB is a different take. It keeps the Alolan Ninetales plus Gengar combination, but here Disable sits on Gengar rather than Perish Song, and Ninetales runs Focus Sash instead of Never-Melt Ice with extra coverage in Blizzard and Freeze-Dry. Even without Perish Song, Gengar plus Alolan Ninetales is still very disruptive thanks to the Disable/Encore combination — splitting the disruption between both Pokemon rather than stacking it on one lets you shut down a target on a given turn and render it useless.

The team leans defensively elsewhere: a classic Shuca Berry Incineroar, and Rotom-Wash running Volt Switch rather than Thunderbolt for extra pivoting — lead Gengar/Rotom, mega evolve and Protect, Volt Switch out, bring in Alolan Ninetales, and suddenly the opponent is in a tricky spot. The Choice Scarf Garchomp here has no Dragon-type attack, running dual Rock/Ground instead — Rock Tomb gets around Wide Guard (like on the tournament-winning Aerodactyl) while also giving some speed control to combo into an immediate follow-up from Gengar or Ninetales. Colbur Berry Aegislash rounds the team out, running Poltergeist, Shadow Sneak, Sacred Sword, and King's Shield — Sacred Sword is valuable coverage into opposing Kingambit and ignores Archaludon's Defense boosts.

Investment leans very offensive on Ninetales, Aegislash, and Garchomp, while Rotom and Incineroar are bulkier support pieces. This Gengar is also notably bulkier and, despite not running Modest, is actually faster post-mega than the Perish Song Gengar from the top 4 team, since both share the same pre-mega Speed.
