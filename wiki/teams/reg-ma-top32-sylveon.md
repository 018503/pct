## Strategy

This team closely resembles the 1st place team, but swaps Mega Floette for Sylveon if you don't happen to have one. It still runs Mega Aerodactyl, though without Wide Guard, and doesn't carry the Scale Shot tech on Garchomp — the core concepts are otherwise quite similar. Sylveon is actually underplayed in the current format given how few strong special attackers exist; a lot of players default to megas like Charizard or Floette, but Sylveon gives you a strong attacker that doesn't need to take up the mega slot at all. Fairy Feather further boosts Hyper Voice, Moonblast, and especially Hyper Beam — a move that can threaten a one-hit knockout on Pokemon that would normally shrug off powerful attacks, even bulky Rotom or Archaludon depending on how they're trained.

The rest of the team leans on ways to boost its own damage output — Soft Sand on Garchomp for Earthquake and Stomping Tantrum, Charizard setting up sun for itself. As on many teams from this tournament, Focus Sash Adaptability Basculegion returns as one of the format's staple picks.

Overall this is a well-rounded team with strong typing coverage that gives Sylveon a real showcase — worth trying if you like the 1st place team's structure but want to avoid committing the mega slot to Floette.
