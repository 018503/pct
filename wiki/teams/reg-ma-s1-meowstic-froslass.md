## Strategy

One of the most unique teams in the video, built around Meowstic, Milotic, and Mega Froslass. Mega Froslass usage has generally been trending down as Mega Charizard has grown more popular, but this build gives it clever support: Froslass runs heavy HP investment with Substitute, and combined with Light Screen and Reflect from Meowstic, it becomes very difficult to knock out — able to sit behind a Substitute and spam Blizzard. Since Froslass struggles against Fire types that resist Blizzard, the team leans on Milotic, Basculegion, and Rain Dance from Meowstic to handle them before Froslass closes games out.

Meowstic is chosen over a more typical pick like Sableye specifically for access to Yawn — a disruptive move that, with Prankster priority, can consistently either put a Pokemon to sleep or force a switch. Milotic runs a supportive Whirlpool + Life Dew set that traps opponents while chipping their HP and healing the team. Sneasler skips both Fake Out and Protect in favor of Throat Chop and Substitute — Throat Chop gives it a surprise attack into Ghost types like Basculegion and Aegislash that normally wall it outright, and also shuts down sound-based moves like opposing Hyper Voice, which matters since this team has no Fairy resistances.

Kingambit again runs four attacks with a Choice Scarf-adjacent speed tier for broad coverage. Milotic and Sneasler both lean defensive — Sneasler in particular runs near-max HP and Defense to survive Dual Wingbeat and Stomping Tantrum from Aerodactyl and Garchomp. Overall the Meowstic / Froslass / Milotic core, especially the Yawn plus Whirlpool plus Substitute combination, was a genuinely novel structure not seen elsewhere this season.
