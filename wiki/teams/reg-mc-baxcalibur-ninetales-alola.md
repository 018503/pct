## Strategy

Mega Baxcalibur is positioned as a counter to the format's most popular mega, Mega Salamence, threatening it with 4x super-effective Ice STAB. The team wraps extensive support around Baxcalibur to compensate for its awkward speed tier and defensive vulnerabilities. Coaching Sneasler immediately boosts Baxcalibur's Attack and Defense, enabling Glaive Rush OHKOs that wouldn't happen unboosted and letting it survive physical hits it otherwise wouldn't. Alolan Ninetales sets Snow for a Special Defense buff and Aurora Veil with Light Clay for extended 8-turn screens, giving Baxcalibur the durability to tank hits while threatening with Glaive Rush.

The team's secondary mode revolves around Life Orb Gholdengo handling the threats Baxcalibur struggles against — steel types like Mega Lucario Z, Mega Golisopod, and opposing Gholdengo. The typical gameplan against teams with these threats is to lead Sneasler plus Gholdengo, use Fake Out to enable a Nasty Plot, then eliminate the Baxcalibur counters before bringing it in to sweep the weakened field. Milotic with Competitive punishes Intimidate users who try to neuter Baxcalibur, and Incineroar provides a second Fake Out, Intimidate cycling, and Parting Shot pivoting. High Horsepower over Swords Dance gives Baxcalibur critical coverage into steel types like Gholdengo, Kingambit, and Archaludon that would otherwise wall it completely.

Key weaknesses include the team's reliance on physical damage from Baxcalibur (making it vulnerable after Glaive Rush's double-damage drawback), faster burst damage from Pokemon like Hisuian Arcanine plus Mega Raichu Y outspeeding and two-shotting it, and Mega Gardevoir plus Psychic Terrain shutting down both Ice Shard priority and Fake Out. The team also lacks strong speed control beyond Icy Wind from Milotic, which can feel slow against aggressive fast-paced leads.

**Role:** Primary sweeper + Salamence counter

Baxcalibur is the team's centerpiece, threatening OHKOs on most of the format after a Coaching boost. Glaive Rush at 120 base power with STAB is devastating, but leaves Baxcalibur taking double damage the following turn — making the Coaching Defense boost and Aurora Veil critical for surviving the retaliation. Max HP at 222 combined with max Speed at 139 lets it outpace Incineroar, Basculegion, Kingambit, and Rillaboom to threaten KOs before they move. Thermal Exchange is retained from the base form, providing an Attack boost if hit by a fire move — situationally useful but rarely activatable safely since fire attacks still deal full damage.

**Role:** Coaching support + Fake Out + anti-steel coverage

Sneasler is Baxcalibur's primary support partner. Coaching immediately gives Baxcalibur +1 Attack and +1 Defense, which is the difference between Glaive Rush 2HKOing and OHKOing many targets and between surviving retaliatory hits after Glaive Rush's double-damage drawback. Fake Out buys a free turn for Baxcalibur to attack or for Gholdengo to Nasty Plot. Close Combat hits Archaludon and Kingambit. Focus Sash over Grassy Seed means Sneasler doesn't need Rillaboom on the team, though it also means Unburden won't activate without taking a hit first. No Protect makes it vulnerable to double-ups — opposing Gholdengo is particularly annoying since Good as Gold blocks Fake Out and Dire Claw, and faster Jolly Sneasler can Fake Out + KO before it moves. Max Attack at 200 and max Speed at 172 ensures it hits hard and moves before most threats.

**Moves:** Muddy Water · Icy Wind · Recover · Protect

**Role:** Anti-Intimidate deterrent + speed control

Milotic punishes Intimidate users with Competitive, getting a +2 SpA boost whenever Salamence, Incineroar, or Staraptor try to debuff Baxcalibur. This creates a team preview dilemma — opponents with Intimidate either avoid bringing it (letting Baxcalibur operate at full power) or bring it and risk Milotic snowballing. Icy Wind provides the team's only reliable speed control, though it's awkward against Kingambit (risking a Defiant boost on switch-in). Max HP/Def with Bold and 147 SpD makes it extremely bulky, and Recover with Leftovers gives it near-infinite staying power. Muddy Water hits both opponents with a chance to lower accuracy. Milotic is best brought when the opponent has Intimidate users; against teams without them, it can feel like dead weight since its damage output is modest.

The key discipline is conserving Incineroar specifically for Golisopod matchups — if it goes down before Golisopod enters, Baxcalibur can get swept by Swords Dance sets.
