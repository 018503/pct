## Strategy

This is another take on Mega Gengar, pairing it with Maushold instead of Alolan Ninetales, and adding unique elements like Encore on Palafin and a Choice Scarf Hydreigon. Choice Scarf Hydreigon is an interesting anti-meta pick — Snarl plus the threat of an immediate Draco Meteor is powerful, and there aren't many strong special attackers in the format to compete with it. The Encore/Disable combination this time runs between Maushold and Gengar rather than Ninetales and Gengar. This Gengar skips Sludge Bomb for Shadow Ball, Perish Song, and Disable, and is both extremely fast and extremely bulky.

The plan is to pivot frequently: lead Palafin or Hydreigon, with Palafin able to switch out and transform from Zero to Hero, then bring in Maushold to support Gengar, trap something in, and go for Disable into Perish Song. Hydreigon crucially runs U-turn, letting it deal damage and pivot rather than hard-switching. Since Perish Song teams want Protect across the board, Incineroar runs Protect here and, notably, Darkest Lariat instead of Flare Blitz — because Pelipper is already on the team for weather control. This Pelipper skips Tailwind for heavy specially defensive investment instead, which is especially valuable against Mega Charizard: trap it with Gengar, bring in Pelipper, and Charizard becomes largely useless.

Wide Guard is one of the best moves in the game, and there's a lot of disruption stacked here between Encore on Palafin, Maushold's own Encore, and Disable plus Perish Song on Gengar. Across the three Mega Gengar teams featured from this tournament, the move sets differ but the core concept — trap, disrupt, and force a Perish Song or chip-damage win — stays the same.
