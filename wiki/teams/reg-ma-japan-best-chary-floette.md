## Strategy

The team a lot of top Japanese players have been using recently, and the one that won the Japanese National Championships outright — the classic Mega Charizard-Y / Mega Floette core, paired with four-attack Roseli Berry Garchomp, Occa Berry Kingambit, and Mystic Water Basculegion. This exact composition has evolved a lot over the format (Fairy Feather Whimsicott, Gliscor, and Incineroar have all been tried in the Garchomp/Kingambit/Basculegion slots at various points), but this is the specific six and spreads that won Japanese Nationals.

Charizard runs slower here, trading speed for a ton of HP and Defense investment to be genuinely bulky. Garchomp, by contrast, has very little bulk — just enough to survive a Dragon Claw from an opposing Garchomp — because its job is four-attack coverage, not staying alive. Kingambit is straightforward, and Basculegion carries some HP and Defense investment specifically to survive opposing Sucker Punches. Floette is only lightly invested in HP and is otherwise modest and near max Special Attack, built to obliterate targets with Light of Ruin.

Kingambit's Iron Head over the more common Low Kick gives it extra Fairy-type coverage, letting it threaten walls that would otherwise stonewall it — combined with Occa Berry to survive opposing Charizard, and four attacks with Roseli Berry on Garchomp, the team can absorb hits for a little longer than most. It isn't a complex build — every single Pokemon just carries a lot of raw offense — but that simplicity, backed by the coverage between Mega Charizard-Y and Kingambit, is exactly what took it all the way to a National Championship win.
