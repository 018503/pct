## Strategy

A team with several techs opponents likely won't expect. Talonflame runs Quick Guard rather than Fake Out, and since Blastoise itself also has no Fake Out, Quick Guard lets the team blunt an opposing Fake Out and then swing Blastoise into whatever's on the field. This Blastoise is a maximum-coverage set — Water Spout, Aura Sphere, Ice Beam, and Dark Pulse — which between Water, Fighting, Ice, and Dark typing hits nearly everything in the format for neutral or super-effective damage.

Archaludon runs the White Herb Sturdy set (as opposed to Choice Scarf), letting a Talonflame + Archaludon lead set Tailwind into Draco Meteor — White Herb clears the Special Attack drop so Archaludon can fire a second Draco Meteor right after. Basculegion runs Waterfall instead of Liquidation, both to add a flinch chance and to avoid triggering an opposing Kingambit's Defiant, since Liquidation's Defense drop would otherwise hand Kingambit a free Attack boost against what's meant to be its own check; the Tailwind-boosted flinch chance from Waterfall is a real bonus here. Garchomp runs Roseli Berry with Rock Tomb, effectively a max Attack / max Speed set that can switch into Sylveon and survive a hit thanks to the berry.

Gardevoir carries an unusual Vacuum Wave rather than the more typical Trick Room, giving it priority coverage into Incineroar and Kingambit — a move the creator notes is rarely expected on Gardevoir. Between Aura Sphere on Blastoise and Archaludon plus Vacuum Wave on Gardevoir, the team runs triple Fighting-type coverage, which lines up well against the number of Fighting-weak Pokemon in the current format. Spreads mostly maximize offense on Archaludon, Basculegion, Garchomp, and Blastoise, while Gardevoir keeps some HP investment to better use its own Tailwind turns.
