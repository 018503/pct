## Strategy

Nick Navar took this to third place at Indianapolis Regionals, leaning into multiple setup Pokemon at once instead of committing to just one sweeper. Calm Mind Floette is joined by Swords Dance Kingambit and Bulk Up Leftovers Palafin — three completely different win conditions the opponent has to respect simultaneously. Double Fake Out from Incineroar and Sneasler buys the setup turns, and Tailwind from Talonflame lets whichever sweeper gets going actually outrun the format.

Because it's setup-oriented, Kingambit and Palafin don't lean on raw speed. Kingambit's game plan is mostly Sucker Punch, so it doesn't need to outrun anything even under Tailwind; Palafin is already fast without investment and Tailwind is more than enough on top. Floette is the Modest max-speed variant with a good chunk of HP and Defense — since this team has no Sinistcha for redirection or passive healing, damage taken while setting up is largely permanent, except on Floette itself, which can heal back to full with Draining Kiss after banking HP loss.

Talonflame's Sharp Beak plus Dual Wingbeat makes it one of the most reliable answers to Focus Sash Whimsicott and Venusaur, both common leads against setup teams; Will-O-Wisp lets it burn physical attackers before they even get to move, which matters a lot when Kingambit, Palafin, and Floette all need a turn of safety to set up. Overall, it's a team that spends the early turns setting up and then closes games out with knockout after knockout once any of its three sweepers gets rolling.
