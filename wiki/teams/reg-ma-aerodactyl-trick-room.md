## Strategy

A top-16 finish at Japanese Nationals with a team that can genuinely play either Tailwind or Trick Room, which makes it very difficult for an opponent to prepare for both. Sylveon and Kingambit look like classic Trick Room pieces, but both actually carry speed investment — Sylveon has 15 speed points and Kingambit is max speed. They're still naturally slow enough to move first under Trick Room against most of the format's attackers, but Aerodactyl's Tailwind unlocks a genuinely faster mode too.

Aerodactyl is flexible on its own — it can be Mega Evolved for extra Dual Wingbeat damage (especially valuable into Mega Venusaur), or run as a non-mega Tailwind support piece next to Mega Kangaskhan instead. That gives two clean team compositions: Kangaskhan / Aerodactyl (non-mega) / Sylveon / Kingambit for the fast mode, or Aerodactyl-Mega / Sylveon / Kingambit plus one more for the higher-damage mode. What looks on paper like a pure Trick Room team is actually able to catch opponents off guard with real speed — Sylveon can outrun Mega Froslass and Sneasler under Tailwind, and Kingambit can catch a lot of opponents off guard entirely.

Focus Sash on Kingambit lets it run a full four-attack coverage set without worrying as much about survivability. Thunderbolt on Farigiraf is a newer tech showing up on Trick Room teams for extra coverage into Mega Charizard and Basculegion. Torkoal, with Eruption in sun plus Helping Hand support, is one of the most effective Trick Room sweepers in the format — but it's generally reserved for the slow mode rather than the faster Tailwind-based one.
