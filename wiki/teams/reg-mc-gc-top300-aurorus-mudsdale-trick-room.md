## Strategy

This is a hard Trick Room team that finished top 300 in the Global Challenge, built around two Pokemon almost nobody uses: **Refrigerate Aurorus** and **Inner Focus Mudsdale**. At team preview it is built to look like something else. Gengar next to Incineroar or Rillaboom looks like Mega Gengar or Perish Song, and Charizard could be Mega Y sun. In practice Gengar is a Focus Sash Trick Room setter and the only mega is Charizard Y. Ice is very good coverage in this format (Garchomp, Salamence, Staraptor, Rillaboom, and Freeze-Dry on Pelipper and Swampert). So under Trick Room, Aurorus's spread Hyper Voice plus Mudsdale's High Horsepower / Rock Slide / Heavy Slam hits almost the whole meta super-effectively.

The usual plan is to lead Gengar with one Fake Out user and set Trick Room on turn 1, then bring in two sweepers from Mudsdale, Aurorus and Charizard. Fake Out goes on whatever threatens Gengar, or the partner attacks if Gengar's Sash is safe. Rillaboom is the lead against Indeedee (Psychic Terrain priority and Imprison Trick Room) because Grassy Surge overrides Psychic Terrain. Every non-Gengar slot runs minimum speed: Quiet Aurorus outspeeds Incineroar and Farigiraf under Trick Room, and Mudsdale has 49 Speed. Mudsdale is the only Pokemon with Protect, because the team is weak to Grassy Glide and Aqua Jet priority. Incineroar runs **Blaze, not Intimidate**, so it doesn't feed Kingambit a Defiant boost. +1 Sucker Punch under Trick Room is a real problem for this team.

James's verdict: the team is one-dimensional and depends on surprise. Once opponents know the sets, they double the Gengar or stop Trick Room, and there is rarely a second Trick Room. Screens with Archaludon / Pelipper rain (Wide Guard stops Hyper Voice and Rock Slide) is the worst matchup. Sylveon / Pelipper Wide Guard and Kingambit are also hard. Aurorus plus Mudsdale was very strong whenever Trick Room went up cleanly.

**Moves:** Sludge Bomb · Shadow Ball · Dazzling Gleam · Trick Room

**Role:** Sash Trick Room setter (Mega Gengar / Perish bluff)

The team's only Trick Room setter. Focus Sash means it usually gets Trick Room up with Fake Out support. At preview it looks like Mega Gengar or Perish Song, so opponents often play around Shadow Tag instead of doubling it. Cursed Body can disable a move that hits it. Quiet lowers its Speed to move a bit earlier under Trick Room, but 117 is still fast, and James found it outspeeding things he wanted it to underspeed. Shadow Ball was key coverage into Gholdengo. If Gengar is the wrong Pokemon to keep in, switch it out under Trick Room, or Grassy Glide your own Gengar to get a free switch.

Lead Charizard with Gengar into weather teams, or when confusion about the mega helps. It's the third choice behind Aurorus and Mudsdale: Glimmora / Garchomp, Gardevoir with Altaria, and Wide Guard Pelipper / Sylveon all gave it trouble.

**Moves:** Flare Blitz · Close Combat · Darkest Lariat · Fake Out

**Role:** Offensive Fake Out support (no Intimidate)

Max HP / max Atk, Brave, minimum Speed. The paste uses only 64 of 66 points. It deliberately runs Blaze instead of Intimidate so Kingambit can't get a Defiant boost. White Herb removes its own Close Combat drops and any Intimidate drops. It's Fake Out support for the Gengar lead, then a real attacker under Trick Room. Opponents who expect bulky Intimidate Incineroar get surprised by the damage: Darkest Lariat nearly took out an Indeedee. Lead it with Gengar into teams without Indeedee, where Rillaboom's terrain isn't needed.

**Moves:** Wood Hammer · Grassy Glide · High Horsepower · Fake Out

**Role:** Fake Out + terrain control, water-type answer

The team's answer to bulky Waters like Milotic, which the rest of the team struggles to damage. Max HP and 25 SpD let it take hits like Indeedee's Expanding Force. Grassy Surge overrides Psychic Terrain, so it's the lead into Indeedee teams: Fake Out, then Grassy Glide on Indeedee while Gengar sets Trick Room. Wood Hammer plus Aurorus's Hyper Voice KO'd Staraptor and Gardevoir, and Grassy Glide cleans up weakened Pokemon late. High Horsepower is there for Glimmora and Fire types: James opened one game with High Horsepower into Glimmora instead of Fake Out. It can also Grassy Glide its own Gengar to bring in a sweeper.

**Moves:** Hyper Voice · Freeze-Dry · Ancient Power · Earth Power

**Role:** Trick Room spread sweeper (Refrigerate Hyper Voice)

Refrigerate turns Hyper Voice into a boosted spread Ice move, and Expert Belt adds more power on super-effective hits. It hits Garchomp, Salamence, Staraptor, Rillaboom and Whimsicott hard, and it can't be redirected. Freeze-Dry OHKOs Pelipper (4x) and hits Swampert. Earth Power covers Archaludon, Gholdengo and Kingambit, and Ancient Power hits Charizard. Quiet minimum Speed (70) underspeeds Incineroar and Farigiraf under Trick Room. The HP / Def points let it survive a Life Orb Garchomp Earthquake. Rock / Ice is a poor defensive typing, so bring it in after Trick Room is up. It swept two of James's games with Hyper Voice. His only complaint is that it doesn't learn Power Gem.

**Moves:** High Horsepower · Rock Slide · Heavy Slam · Protect

**Role:** Physical Trick Room sweeper (Fake Out / Intimidate immune)

Inner Focus blocks flinch and Intimidate, so Fake Out and Incineroar don't slow it down. With 194 Atk and Life Orb, High Horsepower is a huge single-target hit. Rock Slide is spread damage that also handles Charizard. Heavy Slam is for Fairies like Floette and Sylveon. Its coverage hits Delphox, Floette, Sylveon, Charizard and Incineroar. It's the only Pokemon with Protect, so it can stall priority (Grassy Glide, Aqua Jet). Next to Aurorus under Trick Room, the two cover almost everything.
