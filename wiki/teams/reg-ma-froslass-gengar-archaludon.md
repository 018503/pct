## Strategy

Runner-up at the Japanese National Championships, and a genuinely unique mega pairing: Mega Froslass and Mega Gengar together. The standout tech is a surprise Rain Dance on Mega Froslass — against Mega Charizard, especially on ladder, you can lead Froslass behind Archaludon, Protect turn one, then Mega Evolve and Rain Dance to catch the opponent completely off guard. Archaludon then survives the follow-up attack and threatens back with Electro Shot.

It's a dual-mega team where every single Pokemon carries Protect, which also opens up a Perish Song game plan — Incineroar, Politoed, Sinistcha, and Gengar can all lean into a pure Perish mode to trap and knock out a target. Politoed's Hypnosis adds disruption on top, rendering a Pokemon useless for a turn. There are really three distinct modes here: pure Perish, an Archaludon-enabling mode that still leans on Mega Gengar, and the Mega Froslass mode that either Rain Dances immediately or sets Aurora Veil first and then Rain Dances — stacking Veil, Sinistcha healing, and rain support makes Archaludon extremely difficult to deal with.

Archaludon runs Modest nature but essentially no Special Attack investment of its own — the plan is to boost its Electro Shot via the weather rather than raw stats, backed by heavy HP and Special Defense. Froslass is max Speed and max HP rather than offensively invested, since it only carries one attacking move (Blizzard) and exists mainly to set weather and Veil. Mega Gengar runs a ton of HP and Special Defense to survive common special attackers like a Weather Ball Mega Charizard — its Defense is comparatively frail by design, but Incineroar's Intimidate and Froslass scaring away physical attackers like Garchomp cover that gap.
