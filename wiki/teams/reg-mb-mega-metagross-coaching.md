## Strategy

Built by a two-time Taiwanese national champion, this team takes advantage of just how many viable move options Metagross has — everything from Ice Punch and Meteor Mash to Bullet Punch, Psychic Fangs, and even a Rock-type coverage move for Charizard. Rather than the usual attacking set, this one runs Iron Head, Body Press, and Thunder Punch — individually unremarkable moves that become genuinely scary once boosted by Coaching from the Sneasler lead. Even without a Coaching boost, this Metagross can still knock out around a dozen common Pokemon outright and two-hit KO plenty more; Thunder Punch adds coverage against Basculegion and Mega Charizard-Y, and Body Press becomes devastating once Sneasler's Defense boost lands.

It's a dual-mega team, running Calm Mind Floette-Eternal as a secondary win condition — swap it for Sylveon if only running one mega — alongside the classic Incineroar / Sinistcha core that pairs especially well with Calm Mind attackers. Choice Scarf Rotom-Wash provides speed control via Electroweb so Metagross can outrun things that would normally be faster, while also offering Hydro Pump and Volt Switch damage or Will-O-Wisp disruption.

Metagross itself runs enough Speed to outpace max-Speed Garchomp as well as Timid max-Speed Mega Charizard-Y; Rotom-Wash matches that with its own Timid max Speed. Floette-Eternal is built bulky (heavy HP investment) but still Modest max Speed, a common benchmark for outrunning neutral-natured base-Speed Pokemon. Incineroar carries real Speed investment to win the Parting Shot / Throat Chop race against mirror Incineroar, and Sinistcha runs a minus-Speed nature with heavy Defense and Special Defense to lean into the team's Trick Room option.
