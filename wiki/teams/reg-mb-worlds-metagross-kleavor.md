## Strategy

Chloe Bourke brought one of the most unique teams to 2026 Worlds, pairing Mega Metagross and Mega Raichu Y with Kleavor — a Pokemon only four players brought to the tournament. The core theory revolves around Kleavor being incredibly powerful into Mega Charizard: if Charizard leads, Stone Axe punishes it heavily, and if it stays in the back, the stealth rocks from Stone Axe chunk it for half HP on switch-in, often forcing opponents to drop Charizard entirely. With Charizard checked, Metagross has a much easier time operating in the format.

The default lead is Whimsicott + Kleavor in over 75% of matchups. Whimsicott sets Tailwind while Kleavor applies immediate offensive pressure and gets stealth rocks up. Metagross typically operates from the back as the primary mega, cleaning up after Kleavor softens the opposition. Basculegion serves as the endgame closer with heavy defensive investment to survive priority. Fairy Feather Whimsicott is a key innovation — with Kleavor carrying Focus Sash instead, Whimsicott trades survival for significant Moonblast damage, threatening Garchomp and Dragonite harder. Raichu Y flexes over Metagross into rain matchups where Lightning Rod and Zap Cannon (guaranteed accuracy under No Guard) demolish Archaludon and water-types.

Weaknesses include Trick Room (the team is heavily speed-reliant), Stone Axe misses (the swing between hitting and missing is enormous), and Chople Berry Kingambit which walls Metagross and threatens with Kowtow Cleave + Sucker Punch. Positioning is critical since both Kleavor and Metagross can be completely walled by certain common Pokemon — the team compensates through complementary coverage across its members.

Focus Sash guarantees at least one attack, making it an extremely positive trade in the early game. Steel-types completely wall both STAB moves.
